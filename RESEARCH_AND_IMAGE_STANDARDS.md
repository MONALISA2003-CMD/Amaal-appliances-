# Amaal Research & Image Standards

## Research objective
Good catalogue work starts with **exact identity**, then facts, then local market evidence, then a clean image. A product should never be added merely because a similar product exists online.

## Information quality ladder
1. **Manufacturer/OEM source:** primary source for model identity, dimensions, power, capacity, functions and official imagery.
2. **Credible Ugandan retailer/distributor:** primary local evidence for availability, local naming and current selling-price observations.
3. **Regional authorized/credible retailer:** useful when Ugandan evidence is absent, especially for specifications and model confirmation.
4. **Marketplace listing:** discovery/fallback only. It must be cross-checked for exact model identity and misleading specification copy.
5. **Search snippets/social posts:** discovery leads, never final evidence by themselves.

## Exact-model research checklist
- Confirm brand.
- Confirm exact model number and spelling.
- Confirm product family/category.
- Confirm capacity/power and other high-value specifications from a reliable source.
- Collect Uganda price observations when available.
- Compare at least two credible observations when practical.
- Investigate major price conflicts instead of averaging blindly.
- Record source and confidence in the product record.
- Do not merge different models because names or capacities look similar.

## Price research
The master price is a deliberate implementation value, not a scraped number. A retailer promotion does not automatically become Amaal's price. Keep research observations separate from the customer-facing price and document why a value was selected.

## Image research
### Preferred order
1. Clean manufacturer/OEM product image.
2. Clean authorized/regional distributor or credible retailer image.
3. Clean marketplace image only when exact-model identity is confirmed and the image is visually clean.

### Reject an image when
- A seller phone number is visible.
- A retailer watermark or store logo is overlaid on the product image.
- A promotional banner, “sale” badge or price sticker is embedded in the image.
- Another retailer's branding dominates the frame.
- The image is a collage instead of a product photograph when a clean product shot exists.
- The image depicts a different model or an unverified similar model.
- The image is a screenshot of a product page rather than the product image itself.

### Image audit levels
- **URL audit:** HTTPS, allowed host, no obvious product-page URL, no known watermark/promo markers.
- **Visual audit:** human/vision inspection confirms no visible watermark, phone number, retailer branding or promotional overlay.
- **Identity audit:** the pictured product matches the exact model/SKU.

A URL-level pass must never be described as proof of visual watermark-free status.

## Build discipline
Every research addition should leave a trace in the product record: model, source, confidence and image provenance. This keeps future catalogue expansion auditable instead of turning into an untraceable pile of copied listings.
